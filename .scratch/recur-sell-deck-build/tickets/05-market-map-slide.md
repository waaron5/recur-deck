# 05: Market map slide

**What to build:** Slide 3 becomes the real market map. A run chooses two categorical axes buyers actually decide on, selects six to nine competitors plus the target, places every company on the map, writes the subtitle and the callout, and builds the slide: title and subtitle on the left, an L-shaped navy axis pair on a pale tinted band below them, and a full-height navy callout sidebar on the right in white text. There are no quadrant dividers, only the two axis lines.

The judgment has to hold up to a founder reading it. Each competitor currently sells, under the brand shown, a product solving the same core problem for an overlapping buyer, evidenced from its own site; merely adjacent companies, defunct brands, and absorbed brands are out. The mix combines recognised category leaders with peers of similar size. Each axis is two-sided and factual — segment, commitment or delivery model, vertical depth, deployment, pricing model — never a subjective quality or leader score, and at least one axis reflects the target's real differentiator. Every placement, the target's included, is defensible from that company's public material, and competitors are not chosen afterwards to manufacture a flattering quadrant. The target may sit alone only if the callout explains the white space and a competitor shares one of its axis positions.

The callout reads "Our take:" plus one line on where the target wins, then two bullets on market dynamics that criticise categories rather than named companies, then one underlined bullet tying back to a how-we-can-help proposal.

Placement is free: the model gives each company 0–1 coordinates, and code keeps the picture clean without changing the judgment — each logo is a box, overlapping pairs are pushed apart along their smaller overlap with a minimum gap, and every box is clamped inside its own quadrant. Logos are sized to equal optical area rather than a uniform box, capped to a maximum box, capped again by the sharpness rule from ticket 03, and any company whose logo falls below the minimum legible height becomes a text wordmark. The target is marked by a white pill with a thin teal outline behind its logo, and nothing else.

Slide 3's speaker notes carry each competitor's evidence, each placement's reasoning, and the reasoning for both axes.
