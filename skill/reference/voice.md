# Voice

The writing a run's copy has to sound like.

Slides 4-9 are Recur's own introduction, written by Recur, and they ship in every
deck this workflow builds - three slides after the copy you write. Copy that
reads as though a different company wrote it is the defect this file exists to
prevent.

Read this before writing slide 2 or slide 3, and again during the copy review.

## What makes them land

**They commit.** "we know what great looks like", "one size does not fit all".
No hedge, no qualifier, nothing held back for safety. A sentence that could be
quietly withdrawn later does not belong in this deck.

**A label, a colon, then the point.** Three of slide 5's four lines are built
that way, and so is slide 2's own header format. The label names the idea; what
follows has to say something the reader did not already know from the label.

**Idiom, not vocabulary.** "eating the world", "in it for the long haul", "alma
mater", "stewards". Ordinary English used confidently beats the formal synonym
every time.

**Names and numbers carry the claim.** Accel, General Atlantic, Slack,
Atlassian, Crowdstrike. When the specifics do the work, the sentence around them
can stay plain.

**No two sentences are built the same way.** Some are a single clause. Some hang
a second thought off the first - "keeping founder legacy & culture intact", "and
growing their businesses". One has no main verb at all. Six sentences built to
one pattern read as a form, however good each one is by itself. This is the
failure the practice runs actually produced, and it is the one to watch for.

## What not to copy

**The first person.** Slides 4-9 are Recur talking about Recur. On slide 2, "why
we're here" and "why we're excited" are about the target company; only "how we
can help" speaks as Recur. Borrow the cadence, not the subject.

**The length.** These lines sit in boxes wider than slide 2's. A bullet still has
to fit one line of its own box, and the content gate checks that before anything
is built.

**Questions.** Nothing on slides 4-9 asks one, and neither does this deck.
